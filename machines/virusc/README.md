# Access Virus C

Emulation of the Access Virus C desktop module, OS 6.6 (368): plugin `ac-digital-vc`. Not affiliated
with Access.

The real firmware runs from reset: the 80C515's program on an 80C515, which uploads the DSP's program
to a DSP56362 over the host port and plays it from MIDI as on the unit. **Status: beta.**

The panel is the unit's, measured from photos (`docs/panel.md`): its keys go through the firmware's
own key scan, its LEDs show what the firmware lights, at the brightness it drives them (the two LFO
RATE LEDs from the DSP's timers, as on the unit), and the display shows the controller's dots. Knobs drag up and down (shift for fine); `?` lists the
computer keys.

The plugin has the unit's jacks: OUT 1 as its main output, OUT 2 and OUT 3 as two more stereo
outputs (off until the host turns them on; parts and the SECOND OUTPUT reach them as the firmware
routes them), and the two inputs as a stereo side-chain, for the vocoder, the INPUT modes and the
input follower. The inputs work in hosts that feed an instrument's side-chain (Logic, Live, Cubase,
Reaper, Bitwig, FL Studio as VST3, Onyx), not in GarageBand. REAR in the bar under the panel turns the
unit round: the jacks, with cables in the ones in use, and the ON/OFF switch, which works (off, the unit
is silent and dark; on, it boots with its memory as it was).

## ROMs

`virus_c_660x368.bin`, MAME's `virusc` set, BIOS v6.6 (524288 bytes, SHA1
`4c34546889d90522a8d85300c471f18339f88f72`). It is Access's copyrighted work and is not included.
The sound engine is the firmware, so without it the machine has nothing to run.

## Artwork

The panel's brand marks aren't included. Put your own in `artwork/virusc/` in the ROM folder:
`maker` (51.0 x 9.4 mm), `name` (73.4 x 8.0 mm) and `tagline` (76.0 x 3.6 mm), each an SVG or a PNG,
or anywhere in the ROM folder as `ac-digital-vc-maker.svg` and so on.

The display's dot font (`plugin/LcdFont.h`) is drawn for Lemus, not taken from the controller's ROM;
its eight characters of the controller's own RAM (arrows, the MIDI mark) are the firmware's.

The engine is unchanged except for the files left out of the build and one guard: the recompiler
doesn't compile past the end of P memory (`JitBlockChain::create`), where the chip's bootstrap ROM
would be; the board stands in for it (`docs/firmware.md`, "Store to flash"). The flash programming
routine's place in bank 7 came from the dsp563c-tools notes on the Virus firmware
([github.com/dsp563c/dsp563c-tools](https://github.com/dsp563c/dsp563c-tools)); the rest was traced in
the firmware here. The DACs' part number came from Studio Repair's photos of a Virus C's board
([studiorepair.com](http://studiorepair.com/gallery/Access/Virus_C/)). What the machine claims about the
DSP can only be checked against recordings of real units, because Gearmulator's Osirus, the other
emulator using this engine, shares it. Osirus remains an oracle for the 80C515 path, which it replaces
with C++ and this machine doesn't. The LFO RATE LEDs follow Gearmulator, so Osirus can't check them
either.

## Status

Checked four ways; the numbers are in `docs/improvements.md` and `docs/firmware.md`.

- **The firmware against the manual:** every key and LED, STORE and STORE TO FLASH, category search,
  every menu's pages (EDIT, effects, delay, arpeggiator, LFOs, oscillators, filters, SYSTEM), MIDI
  out and MIDI DUMP TX, the power-on keys. The plugin's tests check the store paths, the jacks, the
  tuning and the panel against a session reload.
- **Against Gearmulator's Osirus** (the same DSP engine, its 80C515 replaced with C++;
  `tools/compare/replay.py`): the DSP's program and the uploads of all 768 flash programs the same
  word for word but two bytes that change nothing; notes and every kind of channel message the same;
  in multi mode 15 of 16 parts the same (the 16th, Osirus loads the wrong program). The sound of a
  note on each program: level median +0.00 dB, band shape within what moving the note in time does
  to Osirus itself.
- **Real Virus Cs:** the recordings that exist are phrases on known factory programs (Access's web
  demos, YouTube); E73 PowerStrnJ's chords within 3.5-5.3 dB per third octave with the voicing
  guessed, and an A/B against them by ear passed. No recording has isolated notes on known settings.
- **CPU:** `viruscbench`, busiest workload 5.4-5.6 % of a core at 48 kHz on an M5 Max (budget 8 %),
  every jack on 5.6 %; the DSP's idle loop is skipped exactly, bit-identical with and without.

### Known differences

- The DACs (three Cirrus Logic CS4390s, from photos of the board) are modelled from their datasheet:
  their filter, noise floor and auto-mute. The op-amp stage after them isn't (it isn't in the
  photos), and the inputs reach the DSP without their converter.
- The sample rate, 46,875 Hz (12 MHz / 256), is Gearmulator's figure, not checked on a unit.
- A new instance's RAM banks A and B are copies of flash banks C and D, as a unit fresh from the
  factory might be; what Access put there isn't known.
- STORE TO FLASH: the DSP program that holds the sector while the flash is written is stood in for,
  from what the firmware asks of it; erases finish at once, where the chip takes about a second.
- After a MIDI program change VALUE steps from the program last chosen on the panel, until the
  program view is entered again: the firmware's own, as far as can be told, not confirmed on a unit.
- 12 LEDs are placed by wiring order only (LFO 2's and 3's destinations, BPM).
- The recompiler takes up to about 8 ms the first time code runs that no earlier program used
  (3 blocks over a 2.67 ms deadline across the factory banks, none the second time).

### Not emulated

- The power-on keys: the plugin starts with no key held, so the RAM-clearing combination can't be
  used (State, Reset in the bar under the panel starts a fresh memory instead).
- MIDI THRU: the plugin's MIDI output is the unit's MIDI OUT.

## Tools

`tools/viruscrun` runs the board headless (LCD, notes, a WAV at 46875 Hz).
`tools/viruscbench` is the check set's bench through the plugin's processor (`time`, `golden DIR`,
`check DIR`, `render SCRIPT OUT.wav`) and, on the board alone, every block timed across the factory
programs (`spikes`). `tools/demos` holds the MIDI scripts of the website's clips.
`tools/port_c515.py` regenerates the 80C515's instructions from MAME's source.
